Working Knowledge The emergence of practitioners in late medieval and early modern Europe

WorKnow reconsiders how the Scientific Revolution and the Industrial Revolution emerged together. It reconstructs, in detail and over the long run, how practitioners and their practical knowledge arose in Europe.

Two fields, one question

Why did Europe develop “scientific” and “industrial” revolutions that set it apart from the rest of the world? Historians of science and economic historians have long answered separately. Both fields now point to the same actors: an emerging class of practitioners.

History of science

Studies of the sociological origins of the Scientific Revolution show the role of artisans and other practitioners in joining natural philosophy, mathematisation and experiment. Science appears as a collective, socially embedded process.

Zilsel 1942 · Rossi 1970 · Shapin and Schaffer 1985 · P. H. Smith 2004 · Long 2011

Economic history

Recent work revisits the thesis that practitioners made a key contribution to the Industrial Revolution, with practical mathematics a particularly telling field for linking economic and scientific change.

Cipolla 1969 · Berg 1994 · Mokyr 2002 · Kelly, Mokyr and Ó Gráda 2014 · Kelly and Ó Gráda 2022

What WorKnow does

Read the full project description →
  • Treats practitioners as a social phenomenonMoving beyond studies of individual figures to the emergence of practitioners as a long-run, incremental process.
  • Measures itReconstructing the quantitative features and dynamics of that emergence across late medieval and early modern Europe.
  • Connects the two revolutionsBringing the epistemological weight of practical knowledge into economic history, and economic change into the history of science.
